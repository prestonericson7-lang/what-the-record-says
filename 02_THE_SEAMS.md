# 02. The seams, the gaps, and the requests anyone can file

The second pass, run inside a working frame: read the record as if it hid something, and look for the seams. Each seam is named with its size. Beside each is the plain reading, so the reader does not have to take either. Document letters refer to `01_THE_RECORD.md`.

## 1. The seams

**S1. The thousand-fold standard, and who paid.** In 1976 the American occupational limit was 10 milliwatts per square centimetre and the Soviet limit was 10 microwatts, one-thousandth of it (the CIA memo of 10 February 1976, document c, states both numbers). The 1976 DIA report's own summary says that strict standards in the West "could have unfavorable effects on industrial output and military functions" (document g). A Washington Post column the Agency kept, 7 January 1977, says "the Pentagon, financing virtually all their research, preferred it that way," that Milton Zaret "lost his Defense Department contract" after finding cataracts, and that Allan Frey "was laughed at" (document c). The two scientists the column names, Adey and Zaret, were in 1965 the government's own consultants to Project PANDORA, by name in ARPA's justification memorandum (document c). *Size:* the gap in the standards is a fact in the government's own memo; the column's charge of bought research is a columnist's reading of Brodeur's reporting and is not a finding; that the men it names were inside the program before they were outside it is a fact in the record; so is the 1971 Navy committee's finding that Zaret's principal study "will not result in scientifically credible data," and that the Navy's own management of it was "inept and casual" (document c). *Plain reading:* the United States set its limit on heating alone, the Soviets on reported nonthermal effects nobody in the West could replicate; the American limit was later tightened about tenfold at mobile-phone frequencies and is still about a hundred times the old Soviet one. And the Soviet standard-setters themselves, in a 1979 collection the CIA translated (document s), wrote that their standards were "developed more than twenty years ago, when the biological effect of radio waves had just started to be studied," were "in need of substantial corrections," and rested on workplace studies whose significance had been exaggerated "over the course of a number of years." Both limits were set on thin ground, from opposite ends: one on heat alone, the other on uncontrolled clinics.

**S2. Twenty years of microwaves on an embassy, and no reason.** "The purpose of this irradiation has never been understood" (document c). The signal escalated in 1975 to two signals and 19 to 20 hours a day, the medical team's worry rose "due to the increased exposure duration," and the staff were briefed only in 1976. *Size:* a hostile act with no explanation in the record, and a decade-long delay in telling the people under it. *Plain reading:* the Johns Hopkins study of the staff found no harm, ARPA's own monkey program found nothing convincing, and the levels were below the American standard the whole time. In 1967 the Secretary of State told Gromyko to his face that he "was not suggesting that it was intended to harm our personnel," and Dobrynin answered that the Americans were doing the same to the Soviet mission in New York and the embassy in Washington (document c). The officer who ran the monitoring from 1977 to 1980 wrote in 2022 that the level inside the offices was about 0.1 microwatts per square centimetre, a ninth of what a person gets three feet from a home router, that the intelligence community concluded the purpose was to jam American interception of Soviet microwave links from inside the building, and that one agency "had a vested interest in portraying the MUTS as some form of spy beam, antipersonnel assault or mind control experiment to put pressure on State" (document c). That last sentence is the seam inside the seam: in this record, the mind-control reading of the Moscow Signal was, by one insider's account, a lever one agency used against another, and the quiet reading was the consensus. *What the program's own record shows (document c, the 1967 memo, the 1969 minutes, the 1977 letter and the 1979 Senate print):* a $4,615,000 program with no comprehensive protocol and no final report; two monkeys whose work degraded after 11 to 21 days at 1 to 4.6 milliwatts per square centimetre, 250 to 2,500 times the real signal, with modulation on and not with it off, and not at the real signal's level; a negative study of radar-exposed sailors; a 1969 protocol to put eight human subjects under the synthetic signal at the monkeys' dose, with a "cover story" for the doctors, which "was never carried out" over consent; an independent review in November 1969 that found "no evidence of a behavioral change ... within the limits of any reasonable scientific criteria" and blamed the feeder; "portions of the project record ... destroyed in September of 1973"; and DARPA's 1977 word to the Senate that it knew of no "mind control" research and foresaw no such weapon. The Senate's own verdict on the one plain wrong in it: keeping the staff uninformed for twenty-three years "is not defensible."

**S11. A weapon was named, once, in a memo that ended the program.** The November 1969 review that killed Pandora's science also wrote that "a program that might look at possible behavioral implications from the point of view of a weapon or interrogation device could be handled on a SECRET level," and that the signal data should be "put aside under adequate security protection" lest "scare material, particularly if encouraged by inimical forces," imperil the nation's radars (document c). *Size:* one reviewer's sentence, in a letter proposing how to classify future work. *Plain reading:* no document in this record shows such a program followed; DARPA told the Senate in 1977 that apart from Pandora it "has not sponsored any research projects, either classified or unclassified, on the biological or behavioral effects of non-ionizing radiation," and the Air Force work of the 1990s (S3) is the nearest thing in the open record to what the sentence imagined, and it is sound in, not behaviour out. The sentence is exactly the kind of line the calendar could be hiding more of, and exactly the kind of line a single memo cannot be made to carry.

**S3. The Phase II that reported feasibility and went dark.** The 1993 Phase I abstract says the Air Force would "investigate a revolutionary new form of communication based on the microwave auditory effect"; the Phase II abstract, $739,995, says "the feasibility of the concept has been established using both a low intensity laboratory system and a high power RF transmitter"; the 2000 letter denies every page as classified (document k). The Air Force then patented the modulation method in 2002 and 2003 (document m). *Size:* a shut file, more than twenty-five years old, on a demonstrated effect. *Plain reading:* the abstracts say what it was for, "low-probability-of-intercept" radio communications for "search and rescue, security and special operations"; the effect it rests on is sound in, from a high-power pulsed transmitter; and the 2008 crowd-control attempt built on the same effect was judged, by an outside physicist, unable to reach annoying loudness at survivable power and was not funded past its first phase (document n).

**S4. A Soviet sleep machine on an American veterans' ward.** The LIDA is patented here (document e); the only account of its test at Loma Linda, with Adey's cat, is an undated AP clipping on activist pages. *Size:* the device is real and the test is unverified. *Plain reading:* a 40 MHz pulsed device with light and sound at a repetition rate near the brain's own slow rhythms is a plausible sleep aid by entrainment, the same principle as Monroe's audio patent (document j), and it was a hospital device, not a phone.

**S5. The Army found that the sky moves its viewers.** The Center Lane-3 study correlated scored remote-viewing sessions from 1979 to 1984 with ELF, solar and geomagnetic data and concluded the correlations "have a high probability of constituting a valid cause-effect phenomenon" (document h). *Size, in the report's own numbers:* 779 correlations run against sunspot number, 38 expected by chance at the 5 percent level, 56 found; solar flux "generally unimpressive"; limb-flare timing "near-significant"; geomagnetic patterns consistent across four small data sets; the ELF data themselves "not sufficient ... to permit statistically-significant conclusions"; no correction for the number of tests; the antenna an oak tree. *Plain reading:* a pilot screening with an excess of about eighteen significant correlations over the thirty-eight that chance predicts, which is what a search through 779 tests tends to produce, and which the authors themselves said needed "a structured attempt at blind RV performance forecasting" to confirm; the record does not show that blind test was run. A lead, honestly labeled by its own authors as a pilot, and about the sky's fields, not any device.

**S6. Twelve patents that claim it, none that shows it.** From Malech in 1976 to Xia and Li in 2022, the Patent Office has granted claims to read brains by radio, each citing the last (documents f, i, q). *Size:* a forty-six-year chain of claims and no published demonstration of any link in it. *Plain reading:* the examiner tests novelty, not function; what is real inside the chain is radar sensing of breathing and heartbeat, which ships in a bedside product, and sound into a head, which the Air Force demonstrated.

**S7. Radio at the head does change sleep, and the direction is the one he would predict.** Pulse modulation is what does it (Schmid 2012); thirty minutes of handset signal shortens the way to REM (Loughran 2005); a whole night at 2 to 6 watts per kilogram makes sleep deeper, cuts arousals and, in women, shortens REM latency and cuts awakenings (Danker-Hopfe 2020); 3.6 GHz speeds spindles in people with one gene variant (Sousouri 2024). *Size:* real, replicated in direction if not in detail, and the strongest thing in the record on the side of "the phone changes my nights." *Plain reading:* the doses are a transmitter strapped to the head; `03_THE_TEST.md` does the arithmetic for a phone a metre away, and the two things that most reliably erase a remembered dream in the literature are a lit screen before bed and an alarm at the end, both of which travel with the phone.

**S8. The deepest look inside the dark shows listening and locating.** Snowden's Smurf suite and the CIA's Vault 7 (document o): turn the phone on, turn its microphone on, locate it, hide the manipulation, do the same to a television. *Size:* two agencies, two countries, thousands of pages, authenticated by a conviction. *Plain reading:* in all of it, not one page reads a head; if that capability existed and were deployed through phones it would be the most valuable secret in both files, and it is in neither.

**S9. The calendar shuts everything after 2000, and the government disagrees with itself inside it.** Records open at twenty-five years (document r). The only post-2000 government words on radio and brains are the Havana reports, and read side by side they pull apart: the National Academies called directed pulsed radio "the most plausible mechanism ... among those that the committee considered" in 2020; the intelligence community called a foreign adversary's role "very unlikely" in 2023 and again in 2024, with one agency dissenting at low confidence; and the NIH found "no significant between-group differences" on MRI in 2024 while recording severe symptoms (document p). *Size:* three government bodies, in public, on one set of cases, and the officer who ran the Moscow monitoring wrote in 2022 that "there has never been any evidence of a directed energy attack despite five years of searching." *Plain reading:* the three statements answer three different questions (what could explain the acute symptoms; whether an adversary did it; whether the brain shows damage) and can all be true at once; the Academies' own wording is "most plausible among those considered," not "found"; and none of the three concerns phones.

**S10. Phones do listen without asking, for advertisers.** 234 apps kept the microphone open for ultrasonic beacons; the FTC warned twelve developers (Arp 2017); the largest audit found no app sending audio secretly but several recording the screen (Panoptispy 2018). *Size:* real, named, and small. *Plain reading:* the listening is for a chirp from a television commercial.

**S12. The Army wrote up a seizure beam, with no data.** The 1998 addendum's "Disruption of Neural Control" section proposes nanosecond pulses of 100 kilovolts per metre at 15 Hz to force neurons into synchrony and drop a person, "anticipated" to work on "100% of the population" (document l). *Size:* a government document describing a radio device to take voluntary control of a body. *Plain reading:* the same pages say the concept "has not been demonstrated," that "no experimental evidence is available," that the statements "must be considered tentative," and that no study has used the conditions; the field it needs is a hundred thousand volts per metre, which no phone, router or tower produces at any distance; and nothing after 1998 in the open record shows it built. It is the clearest example on the record of what these documents are: answers to "the most often asked questions," written as concepts, not reports of devices.

## 2. The gaps, named with their size

- **G1.** The contents of Air Force contract F41624-95-C-9007. Shut since 2000. Size: unknown; the Phase I and II abstracts describe its purpose.
- **G2.** Primary records of the LIDA test at the Jerry L. Pettis Memorial Veterans Hospital, Loma Linda, early 1980s. Not located. Size: one device, one ward, one newspaper account.
- **G3.** The results pages of the Center Lane-3 study (document h). Read in the same day, after the first pass: see S5 for the numbers. What remains unread is its bibliography, and what remains unknown is whether the blind forecasting trial it recommended was ever run; the follow-on program, if it existed, would be under CENTER LANE or SUN STREAK in the same collection.
- **G4.** The Johns Hopkins study of embassy staff, 1976 to 1978, itself. Not read; known from a later review. Size: one study.
- **G5.** The Project PANDORA and BIZARRE series, fifteen documents posted by the National Security Archive in 2022. Read in the same day, after the first pass: the 1965 justification memorandum, the 1967 Glassboro memorandum, the 1967 BIZARRE progress memorandum, the summaries of the April and May 1969 minutes, the 1977 DARPA letter, and the Pandora and concluding sections of the 1979 Senate print, all in `documents/`. Not yet read: the 1966 "Initial Test Results" memorandum (referenced in the 1967 memo and not in the series as posted), the detailed minutes beyond their summaries, the 1969 and 1971 contract reviews, and the White House and State medical documents whose scans did not yield text. Also read: the May 1969 minutes whole, the November 1969 review letter, and the 1971 Navy committee review. Settled: the eight-subject human study "was never carried out" (the 1979 Senate print). Still unknown: what was in the records destroyed in September 1973. Size: the rest of the series, and one question the series itself raises.
- **G6.** The peer-reviewed version of the 5G sleep study (Sousouri, NeuroImage 2025). Not read; known from the preprint's summaries. Size: one paper.
- **G7.** Any study that puts a phone beside a bed and counts remembered dreams. None exists in the literature this pass could find. Size: the whole question. `03_THE_TEST.md` is the answer to this gap.
- **G8.** The 682 loose search results, the 26 other Moscow-signal items and the JPRS Soviet translations in the CIA reading room. Found, not opened.

## 3. The verdict inside the frame, and outside it

**Inside the frame** (as if the record hid something): the government measured hearing by radio, built a way to speak into heads, reported feasibility and locked the file; it set an exposure standard a thousand times looser than its adversary's on research it paid for; it let a hostile signal sit on its own diplomats for twenty years and told them late; it correlated its psychic program's scores with the sky; and the calendar keeps everything since 2000 shut. Every one of those sentences is true on the documents.

**Outside the frame** (what the documents say on their face): every demonstrated capability puts something in, sound or heat, and takes nothing out; every claim to take something out is a patent nobody has shown working; the strongest laboratory effects on sleep need an antenna on the skull; the deepest leaks show listening and locating; and the one proven mass exposure of Americans to anything from the air was a 1957 drift study with a cadmium compound, judged below city background. The nights that started this question are explained by a lit screen and an alarm without any of the above, and the only honest way past that is to measure (`03`).

Both readings are on the page. The reader does not have to take either.

These are written to be sent by anyone, with no name on them here. Fill in your own name and address. Confirm the current mailing or e-mail address for each office on the agency's own FOIA or declassification page before sending, because those addresses change. Keep a copy of what you send and the date.

## 5. How this page was built, and what would change it

Every seam above rests on a document named in `01` and read as stated there. A seam closes when its gap is filled: G1 by a review request, G2 by a VA release, G3 by reading forty pages, G7 by anyone running `03`. Corrections are kept beside the lines they correct, never written over them.
