# 01. The record, document by document

The first pass, 19 November 2026. The method: the government's own documents first, found by program name, contract number and document number, and quoted from their own pages; the patents read from the Patent Office's own scans; the laboratory record beside them, from the papers' own abstracts; the working frame and the plain reading kept apart; and at the end, a measurement anyone can run. Every document named here was read as stated in section 6; nothing is cited from a summary of it without saying so. Every document's number, holder and link, and the copies held in this repository, are in `documents/INDEX.md`; the Patent Office scans of the twelve patents' first pages are in `documents/patents/`.

**The search problem.** The CIA reading room's search box answers only in a browser and answers best to exact phrases and names. "Microwave hearing" loose returns 687 items, almost all 1976 newspaper clippings; the exact phrase returns five, three of which are one real document. The Patent Office serves its scans only as downloads with no text layer. Two European and commercial patent sites put bot checks in the way, and PubMed put a cookie wall in front of its abstracts; each time the agency's own data service answered instead. The documents that matter sit under the code names (PANDORA, BIZARRE, TUMS, CENTER LANE, STARGATE), the contract prefixes (F41624, Brooks Air Force Base), and the document numbers (DST, ST-CS, CIA-RDP). That is how everything below was found.

## 1. The government's record, in the order it was written

**(a) 1957 to 1958, Operation LAC and the city tests. National Research Council, "Toxicologic Assessment of the Army's Zinc Cadmium Sulfide Dispersion Tests," 1997 (read: the summary and exposure chapters as served by the National Academies Press and the NCBI Bookshelf; not read whole).** The Army Chemical Corps dispersed zinc cadmium sulfide particles over Minneapolis, St. Louis, Corpus Christi, Fort Wayne, Winnipeg and much of the country to study how a cloud drifts. The subcommittee's estimates of the highest inhaled cadmium dose: Minneapolis 6.8 micrograms, Winnipeg 14.5, St. Louis 24.4, Biltmore Beach, Florida 390 (unpopulated at the time). Its conclusion: exposures "did not exceed the background exposures encountered in urban areas" except at Biltmore Beach. Critics, Leonard Cole among them, dispute the reassurance. This is the one documented case of the government dusting its own cities with a metal compound, and it is in a government-commissioned report, not a rumor.

**(b) 1962, Allan H. Frey, "Human auditory system response to modulated electromagnetic energy," Journal of Applied Physiology 17:689 (not read; cited by document (l) and patent (i)).** The first paper on hearing pulsed radio inside the head.

**(c) 1965 to 1979, the Moscow Signal and Projects PANDORA, BIZARRE and TUMS.**
- *National Security Archive posting of 13 September 2022, "Microwave Mysteries: Projects PANDORA and BIZARRE" (read whole, the posting; the underlying documents listed there not opened).* Soviet intelligence "bathed the U.S. Embassy in Moscow with microwave transmissions on a daily basis, and, in the late 1950s, penetrated the U.S. ambassador's residence with ionizing radiation." ARPA's Richard Cesaro, September 1967, Top Secret: "The Soviets have reported in the open literature that humans subjected to low-level (non-thermal) modulated microwave radiation, show adverse clinical and physiological effects ... The ARPA BIZARRE program will establish methods which should permit us to relate the behavior of sub-human primates to man under conditions of microwave exposure. This may require direct testing with humans under controlled conditions." Cesaro's October 1965 "Justification Memorandum for Project PANDORA" says the White House ordered "intensive investigative research" under the code name TUMS, Technical Unidentified Moscow Signal.
- *ARPA, "Justification Memorandum for Project PANDORA," 15 October 1965, ARPA-AS-TS-65-23, from the Acting Director, Advanced Sensors, to the Director of ARPA, Top Secret, 2 pages (read whole; `documents/ARPA_Cesaro_Justification_Memorandum_Project_PANDORA_15-Oct-1965.pdf`).* "For several years it has been noted that the American Embassy in Moscow has been radiated with low level electromagnetic signals on a more or less continuous basis. These emitting signals, in the 'S' and 'L' band spectrum, have been of complex modulation with seemingly random variations. The White House has directed through USIB that intensive investigative research be conducted within the State Department, CIA and DoD to attempt to determine what the actual threat is and stop it. The National program has been coordinated by the State Department under code name project 'TUMS'." ARPA's part: "to irradiate a group of primates under carefully controlled conditions simulating the dosages and complex modulation of the threat ... known as Project PANDORA," with the Air Force providing the generating equipment, the Applied Physics Laboratory the laboratory design, and "the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research ... laboratory quarters, primates, biological monitoring." Consultants named: "Drs. Nat Baldwin, NIH; Ross Adey, UCLA; Milton Zaret, Zaret Foundation; J. Johnson, CIA; and H. Pollack, IDA." The two scientists the 1977 column says were punished for finding harm were, in 1965, the government's own consultants on the program.
- *Department of State, Memorandum of Conversation, "Signals against Embassy Moscow Chancery," Glassboro, New Jersey, 25 June 1967, Secret/Nodis, with Ambassador Thompson's covering letter of 11 July 1967 (read whole; `documents/State_Memcon_Glassboro_Signals_Against_Embassy_Moscow_25-Jun-1967.pdf`).* The Secretary of State to Foreign Minister Gromyko and Ambassador Dobrynin: "we were very much concerned at an electromagnetic signal directed against our Chancery building in Moscow. The radiation from this signal was of a level higher than Soviet experts considered safe. We did not know the purpose of this activity and he was not suggesting that it was intended to harm our personnel but he asked that the matter be investigated and the activity stopped." Gromyko "expressed skepticism." Dobrynin "said there was similar activity directed both against the Soviet Mission in New York as well as the Soviet Embassy in Washington."
- *ARPA, "Project BIZARRE," progress memorandum for the Director of Defense Research and Engineering, 27 September 1967, ARPA-AS-TS-67-20, Top Secret (read whole; `documents/ARPA_Project_BIZARRE_progress_memorandum_27-Sep-1967.pdf`).* Its summary: "The central nervous system of primates has been penetrated directly or indirectly by non-thermal modulated microwave radiation at power density levels from 4.0 milliwatts per square centimeter down to 1.0 mw/cm2 (these levels are below present U.S. safety standards). At similar radiation power densities and non-modulated low microwave radiation the primates performed normally. The recent low level BIZARRE tests at .008, .05, .01 mw/cm2 power densities under modulated microwave radiation did not cause the primate to degrade in conducting his work tasks. The latest data collected on the Moscow data indicates energy densities no greater than .05 mw/cm2 in the Moscow Signal." And: "The intent of the 'Moscow Signal' has not been established by the experiments conducted to date." Modulation mattered and the Moscow level did not degrade the animals, in ARPA's own 1967 words.
- *"Minutes of Pandora Meeting of April 21, 1969" and "of May 12, 1969" (read: the summary pages; `documents/Minutes_of_Pandora_Meeting_21-Apr-1969.pdf` and `_12-May-1969.pdf`).* The "Big Boy" study of the USS Saratoga's crew, radar-exposed against sheltered: "No significant differences in psychological tests performed on the apparently exposed and control groups. Also, no apparently significant differences on genetic (leucocyte) and physical findings. Thus, studies generally proved negative"; shipboard levels "from 0.3 to 0.03 mw/cm2 and in no case greater than 1 mw/cm2."
- *The May 12, 1969 minutes, read whole.* The protocol for the human study, as the Science Advisory Committee discussed it: ninety days, eight subjects, built on the assumption "that there is an effect of the signal," using "the special signal at the levels developed and used with the primates, i.e., between 4.5 and 5 mw/cm2," beamed at the back of a seated subject with "absorbent seat and gonadal protection," eye protection "considered," slit-lamp, visual-field and hearing checks at 90 and 180 days, a weekly electrocardiogram and physical, and a psychiatric evaluation before and after; "DOD regards the general line of effort to acquire human-based data on effects of the signal, with appropriate safeguards, as a high priority. ARPA believes that the entire effort should be classified"; "An appropriate cover relates to the purpose of the program to evaluate the validity of U.S.S.R. reports that nonthermal effects of nonionizing radiation are significant"; and a "medical monitoring task force" that "would not be privy to Pandora but would be given the cover story." The minutes also record "the morphological changes (cytological) which have been found in the CNS of animals exposed to the signal appeared in the visual cortex."
- *"Review of Project Pandora Experiments," letter to Dr. Stephen Lukasik, Deputy Director of ARPA, 4 November 1969, WL-613, initialed "SK," downgraded to unclassified 24 September 1973 (read whole; `documents/Review_of_Project_Pandora_Experiments_letter_to_Lukasik_4-Nov-1969.pdf`).* After "approximately 8 hours (October 22, 1969) looking at the material": "I am forced to conclude that the data do not present any evidence of a behavioral change due to the presence of the special signal within the limits of any reasonable scientific criteria. There is evidence of behavioral change in some cases but this change could be attributed to a variety of causes or systematic measurement errors all well within the limits of experimental methodology." Four operant-conditioned animals, exposed seven times to the special signal; "The intensity generally used has been 4.6 mw/cm2 which, I might point out, is probably in excess of the special site environment"; the degradations "well within the limits of causes other than radiation such as change of the animal from one room to another, day/night variations, or perturbations caused by malfunction of equipment," with "a considerable number of malfunctions in the pellet-feeding gear"; "no trends observed." Then the reviewer's policy worry and proposal: "Failure to have absolute scientific evidence of the presence or absence of an effect and its threshold region can leave the U.S. vulnerable to a campaign against the use of surveillance radars, foreign and domestic, military and civilian, as well as high-powered communications equipment. A possible public and consequent Congressional reaction on scare material, particularly if encouraged by inimical forces, could result in a catastrophic impediment to the use of various equipments essential for the national security"; the compartmented signal data "should be put aside under adequate security protection"; "A program that might look at possible behavioral implications from the point of view of a weapon or interrogation device could be handled on a SECRET level. The more pressing issue is the safety problem and that could be handled on a CONFIDENTIAL or OUO level." The initials are consistent with Samuel Koslov, the ARPA scientist whose 1965 memorandum the National Security Archive credits with proposing the primate tests; that identification is an inference, not a reading. Pandora was terminated four months later.
- *Ad hoc committee review of Navy contract N00014-69-C-0358, "Radio-Frequency Hazards," principal investigator Dr. Milton M. Zaret, letter to Harry Sonnemann, Office of the Assistant Secretary of the Navy (R&D), 16 August 1971 (read: the general findings; `documents/Navy_ad_hoc_review_of_Zaret_contract_N00014-69-C-0358_16-Aug-1971.pdf`).* "Long-term, low-level exposure of primates to microwave is a worthwhile and necessary experimental effort"; but "The principal study in concept and execution has not utilized adequate techniques or proper scientific methodology and will not result in scientifically credible data," and "it is doubtful whether valid research on this problem would be performed in the future by the same personnel"; the smaller rabbit study at Brooklyn Polytechnic "has employed adequate techniques"; and "the Navy shares responsibility for the poor showing ... the Navy clearly did not supply a source of adequate intensity ... inept and casual management was demonstrated by the Navy organization involved." This is the other side of the 1977 column's line that Zaret "lost his Defense Department contract": a peer committee found his main study not credible, and found the Navy's handling of it inept.
- *DARPA to Senator Warren G. Magnuson, Chairman of the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation, 15 September 1977 (read whole; `documents/DARPA_to_Senator_Magnuson_on_Project_Pandora_15-Sep-1977.pdf`).* "Project Pandora was classified at the direction of the Department of State and the United States Intelligence Board because of the sensitivity of the radiation problem of the United States Embassy in Moscow at that time"; "The total cost of Project Pandora was $4,615,000"; "This agency has no knowledge as to whether the data collected at Walter Reed were destroyed"; "Project Pandora was terminated by DARPA in March 1970"; "With the exception of Project Pandora, this agency has not sponsored any research projects, either classified or unclassified, on the biological or behavioral effects of non-ionizing radiation"; "This agency is not aware of any research projects, classified or unclassified, conducted under the auspices of the Defense Department, now on-going or in the past, which would have probed possibilities of utilizing microwave radiation as a form of what is popularly known as 'mind control'"; "We do not foresee the development, by DARPA, of weapons using microwaves and actively being directed toward altering nervous system function or behavior. Neither are we aware of any of our own forces or possible adversary forces developing such weapons." That is DARPA's statement to Congress; it is also the agency that, eleven months earlier, the record shows reading the same question about its own program.
- *United States Senate, Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation, committee print, "Microwave Irradiation of the U.S. Embassy in Moscow: review of its history and studies to determine whether or not related health defects were experienced by employees assigned in the period 1953-1977," 1979 (read in part: the Pandora section, the Johns Hopkins section's scope, and the committee's five concluding questions; `documents/Senate_Commerce_Committee_Print_Microwave_Irradiation_of_the_US_Embassy_in_Moscow_1979.pdf`).* On Pandora: the laboratory signal "yielded an exposure intensity of between 4 and 5 milliwatts per square centimeter compared to the average intensity of a Moscow signal which generally measured between 2 and 18 microwatts per square centimeter"; "The record of what was done during the course of this program is scattered and incomplete"; "There existed no comprehensive protocol for the conduct of the experimental program"; "The results of these studies were ambiguous and controversial. The experimental methodology led to inconclusive results. No comprehensive project report was ever prepared. Portions of the project record consisting of certain classified documents were destroyed in September of 1973, about 2 years after the project was terminated." The principal investigator, Major Joseph Sharp, reported "two monkeys exposed to the 'Special' Moscow signal at intensities from 1 to 4.6 milliwatts per square centimeter" showing "degradation in previously learned work performance after 11 to 21 days of such exposure at 10 hours per day," reversible, with a hint of cumulation; "The foregoing findings were not supported" by the later work. The Science Advisory Committee found "the impossibility of drawing any reliable conclusions without additional scientific study," and "in its meeting of May 12, 1969, SAC reviewed WRAIR's proposed protocol for a classified study involving eight human subjects to be exposed to the same synthetic 'Moscow' signal," with independent medical and psychiatric monitoring. It was never run: "Its extension to proposed experimentation with humans was never carried out because of the DOD's concern about ill-defined requirements then emerging for fully informed and consenting subjects for human experimentation," the committee having noted that "grave questions as to the ethical acceptability of such studies were still unanswered." On the Johns Hopkins study (Abraham Lilienfeld, June 1976 to 20 November 1978, all agencies' employees and dependents in Moscow 1953 to 1976 against the other Eastern European embassies): the exposure "never exceeded about 18 microwatts per square centimeter which is far below the U.S. voluntary standard," so the study "contributes little to answering the question as to the level, if any, for such a standard." The committee's judgments: that for "over a decade from the time of initial discovery in 1953 ... nothing of a medical or scientific nature was done"; that withholding the fact of the radiation from employees for twenty-three years "is not defensible, regardless of the outcome of the studies. The employees should have been promptly informed"; that "Requests to the U.S.S.R. to stop the irradiation of the U.S. Embassy were ignored"; and that the purpose "was not included in this study as it has no bearing on the issue of health." A footnote: the fact "did leak and was reported in the media in 1972."
- *CIA-RDP78M02660R000800070028-4, "Moscow Signal Activity," dated 10 February 1976, forwarded 13 February 1976 by the CIA's Deputy Legislative Counsel to Kempton Jenkins of the State Department, 3 pages, Secret, released 2004 (read whole, from the reading room's text).* "The Soviets have been irradiating the U.S. Embassy, Moscow for more than 20 years. The purpose of this irradiation has never been understood. Approximately a decade ago medical research was instituted to determine whether the signals were harmful. That research did not produce significant indications of hazard. Signal strengths, from their inception until May of 1975, were very low, below the Soviet standard of 0.01 milliwatts per sq. cm. for an 8 hour day and therefore much less than the generally used 10.0 mW per sq. cm. industrial standard established in the U.S." A stronger signal in May 1975; a medical scientist sent in July found "the probability was very low that a hazard existed from the levels then measured: 0.01 mW per sq. cm. for 8 to 10 hours per day"; then a second signal and "an increase in signal duration to 19 to 20 hours per day"; a second team found "the possibility of the existence of a health hazard was increased due to the increased exposure duration, certain changes in signal characteristics, and other medical considerations"; the State Department "instituted an effort to get the signal shut off and resolved to brief all embassy staff on the health hazard possibilities."
- *CIA-RDP88B01125R000300120036-2, Stephen S. Rosenfeld, "Radiation Sickness: Medical and Political," Washington Post, 7 January 1977, kept in the Agency's files (read in part, the first two-thirds).* A newspaper column, not a finding. It reviews Paul Brodeur's New Yorker series and says: the American standard was ten milliwatts per square centimetre and the Soviet standard ten microwatts, "one-thousandth as much"; "The Pentagon, financing virtually all their research, preferred it that way"; Milton Zaret, who found cataracts, "lost his Defense Department contract"; "Allan H. Frey, doing pioneer research on the effects of microwaves on the nervous system, was laughed at"; the ambassador "reportedly suffered nausea and bleeding in the eyes"; and "no one sought to refute Anderson's claim that the Russians were trying to alter the minds and behavior of American diplomats."
- *The embassy health study (secondary: the Australian radiation-safety agency's review of the Johns Hopkins study of 1976 to 1978, and a 1988 AP retrospective; the study itself not read).* No adverse health effects tied to the signal were found; the 1988 piece says questions about the study's validity remained.
- *Jon LeChevet, "Regarding Moscow Signal," a letter in the Foreign Service Journal, March 2022, posted by the National Security Archive (read whole; a first-person account, not a government record, by the State Department officer "personally responsible for this mission from 1977 to 1980," a physicist who retired from the Senior Foreign Service in 2005).* The State Department "investigated and documented the Moscow Unidentified Technical Signals (MUTS) from 1973 until the signal disappeared (by 1992)"; "the intensity of the impinging signals were kept at or below a level of 10 microwatts per square centimeter during this time. This level was agreed to by the Soviets because it was their maximum exposure level"; excursions "by a factor of two or three, at most, and for short periods," which fell when protested; the building cut the level "by a factor of 10 to 100," copper screens on the windows from 1976 to "about 1 percent," so that "people working on the higher floors of the embassy were exposed to a level of about 0.1 microwatts per square centimeter"; for scale, "a person standing three feet from a Wi-Fi router could be exposed to a radiation level of 0.9 microwatts per square centimeter" and "a person using a cell phone could have the central portion of the brain exposed to a level of over 1,000 microwatts per square centimeter." On the purpose: "there is overwhelming evidence that the purpose of the MUTS was to disrupt potential interception of sensitive microwave communications by covert signal intelligence operations that the Soviets surmised were operating within the embassy. This was the conclusion of the IC and was supported by all members save one agency. This particular agency had a vested interest in portraying the MUTS as some form of spy beam, antipersonnel assault or mind control experiment to put pressure on State to force the Soviets to shut down the MUTS operation." On Havana: "there has never been any evidence of a directed energy attack despite five years of searching by State, the IC and others." A letter is one man's account; it is the only account in this record from the person who held the meter.

**(d) July 1972, DIA, ST-CS-01-169-72, "Controlled Offensive Behavior, USSR," by Captain John D. LaMothe, Army Medical Intelligence Office, declassified 2003, CIA-RDP96-00788R001300020001-6 (read: the table of contents, Part X whole, and every sentence containing the words telepathy, electromagnetic, radio, sleep, microwave, apparatus and million, from the Internet Archive's full text; not read whole).** Its own words: the techniques the Soviets studied "include biochemicals, sound, light, color, odors, sensory deprivation, sleep, electronic and magnetic fields, hypnosis, autosuggestion, and paranormal phenomena (psychokinesis, extrasensory perception, astral projection, dream state, clairvoyance, and precognition)"; "The Soviets prefer the term biocommunications instead of the term parapsychology"; "twenty or more centers for the study of parapsychological phenomena, with an annual budget estimated in 1967 at over 12 million rubles (13 million dollars) and reported to be as high as 21 million dollars." Part X, Electromagnetic Effects, pages 107 to 109, is the whole of what the report has on radio and the brain: a 1 megavolt test pulse that knocked a bird out of the air, which "flew off about its avian business apparently none the worse"; Salvignac's 1967 report of "slowing of thought processes and psychomotor reactions" in aircrews struck by lightning; Kholodov's 455-entry book on fields and the central nervous system; and the judgment that the Soviets "have considerable knowledge and a keen interest in the field." The word microwave does not appear in the report.

**(e) US Patent 3,773,049, Lev Yakovlevich Rabichev and six co-inventors, Kishinev, USSR; filed 13 November 1970, granted 20 November 1973 (first page read from the Patent Office scan).** "Apparatus for the treatment of neuropsychic and somatic diseases with heat, light, sound and VHF electromagnetic radiation": pulses of light, sound, VHF radio and heat "simultaneously applied by means of a control unit to the patient's central nervous system with a predetermined repetition rate," the light and sound made "monotonous" to the visual and auditory analyzers. This is the LIDA machine. Secondary accounts (an undated Associated Press clipping reproduced on activist pages) put its carrier at about 40 MHz and about 40 watts, say one was loaned to the Jerry L. Pettis Memorial Veterans Hospital at Loma Linda in the early 1980s through a medical exchange, and quote W. Ross Adey that a cat under it was "sitting there very quietly" within two or three minutes. The patent is primary; the test accounts are secondary and are marked so.

**(f) US Patent 3,951,134, Robert G. Malech, assigned to Dorne & Margolin Inc., Bohemia, New York; filed 5 August 1974, granted 20 April 1976 (first page read).** "Apparatus and method for remotely monitoring and altering brain waves." Abstract: "electromagnetic signals of different frequencies are simultaneously transmitted to the brain of the subject in which the signals interfere with one another to yield a waveform which is modulated by the subject's brain waves. The interference waveform which is representative of the brain wave activity is re-transmitted by the brain to a receiver where it is demodulated and amplified ... The demodulated waveform also can be used to produce a compensating signal which is transmitted back to the brain to effect a desired change in electrical activity therein." It cites the LIDA patent. No prototype, trial, measurement or physics analysis of it has been published that this search could find; later patents cite it as prior art and none cites a working unit. The Patent Office examines a claim for novelty and written description, not for whether it works; that caveat applies to every patent on this page.

**(g) March 1976, DIA, DST-1810S-074-76, "Biological Effects of Electromagnetic Radiation (Radiowaves and Microwaves), Eurasian Communist Countries," Adams and Williams, Army Medical Intelligence and Information Agency (read: the summary, the central-nervous-system passages and the antipersonnel passage, from a full transcript; the scan not opened).** Its summary: "The thermal effects of electromagnetic radiation have been reasonably well established through experimental investigation. The nonthermal effects, however, remain a controversial issue between scientists in the West and in the Eurasian Communist countries." It reports Soviet occupational studies (37 people exposed at 10 microwatts per square centimetre for two to eight years, "symptoms of asthenic and autonomic vascular disturbances, endocrine shifts, and abnormal EEG's ... in half of the patients"), rabbit EEG changes at 40 microwatts per square centimetre, and, in its forecast, antipersonnel possibilities: sounds in the head, heart seizure "accomplished experimentally in frogs by synchronizing a pulsed ultrahigh frequency microwave signal of low average-power density with the depolarization of the myocardium," and alteration of the blood-brain barrier. A review of other people's papers plus forecasting; it measures nothing itself.

**(h) 15 November 1983 to 15 December 1984, "Center Lane-3, Geophysical Effects Study," SRI International for Army INSCOM, Marsha Adams and Harold E. Puthoff, 53 pages, Secret, Center Lane Special Access Program, CIA-RDP96-00788R001800270001-4, released 2000 (read whole from the reading room's text, except the references and bibliography).** Objective: "to investigate the possible effects of ambient geophysical/extremely low-frequency electromagnetic factors on remote viewing (RV) performance as a potential aid to increasing the performance levels of Army INSCOM remote viewers." Two ELF monitoring stations (0 to 30 Hz) at SRI Menlo Park and a Los Altos field station 17 km away, the antenna at each "an oak tree" ("the oak-tree bioantenna"), checked against a coil antenna lent by Stanford; solar flux, sunspot number, flares, geomagnetic indices and ionospheric disturbances by NOAA satellite downlink; four to five sets of scored sessions from 1979, 1980, 1981 and 1984. Its own numbers: for sunspot number, "Overall, 779 correlations were performed. Up to 38 correlations at the p = 0.05 level would be expected to occur by chance. Fifty-six correlations were observed, or about 50 percent over what would be expected by chance," with clusters 27 to 30 days and 16 to 19 days before sessions and two to four days after; for solar flux, "the relationships found by epoch analysis in these data were generally unimpressive" and "there was great variability between the individuals, and no definable pattern emerged"; for limb flares, more on low-scoring days, "near-significant" before high-scoring days; for the geomagnetic index Kp, "the geomagnetic field was quiet just before all high-scoring sessions, then became more active the day of the session," a pattern "replicated in the majority of the test data sets"; for ionospheric disturbances, "nine of ten of the data sets had an SES-type SID occurring on the day of the session"; for ELF itself, "there were not sufficient data of sufficient definition, however, to permit statistically-significant conclusions to be drawn," and a later "apparently consistent pattern" of high scores during a rapid rise in ELF, from four cases each. Its conclusions: the correlations "are significant, and have a high probability of constituting a valid cause-effect phenomenon"; "this pilot study provides evidence that the quality of RV functioning may be intimately related to the geophysical environment"; and the recommended next step was "a structured attempt at blind RV performance forecasting." No correction for the number of tests is reported anywhere in it. Microwave appears only in the literature search. This is the Army screening hundreds of correlations between the sky's own fields and its viewers' scores, finding half again as many at the 5 percent level as chance would give, and calling for a blind test that this record does not show was ever run. It is about natural fields, not devices.

## 2. The laboratory record

Three abstracts were read whole from the National Library of Medicine's data service; the rest are from abstracts and summaries as marked.

- **Loughran, Wood, Barton, Croft, Thompson, Stough, NeuroReport 16:1973, 2005 (abstract read whole).** "Fifty participants were exposed to electromagnetic fields for 30 min prior to sleep. Results showed a decrease in rapid eye movement sleep latency and increased electroencephalogram spectral power in the 11.5-12.25 Hz frequency range during the initial part of sleep following exposure. These results are evidence that mobile phone exposure prior to sleep may promote rapid eye movement sleep and modify the sleep electroencephalogram in the first non-rapid eye movement sleep period." A conference write-up of the same work (secondary) puts the REM-latency change at 16 percent, p = 0.02, "marginal" after correction for multiple comparisons.
- **Huber et al., 2002 (abstract only).** Pulse-modulated 900 MHz exposure raised alpha and spindle-range power; unmodulated exposure did not.
- **Danker-Hopfe et al., American Journal of Human Biology 2010 (secondary summaries).** A double-blind, sham-controlled field study with an experimental base station in ten small German villages for two weeks each: no effect of real versus sham exposure on recorded sleep; complaints tracked belief, not exposure.
- **Schmid, Loughran, Regel, Murbach, Bratic Grunauer, Rusterholz, Bersagliere, Kuster, Achermann, Journal of Sleep Research 21:50, 2012 (abstract read whole).** "Thirty young healthy men were exposed, at weekly intervals, to three different conditions for 30 min directly prior to an 8-h sleep period. Exposure consisted of a 900-MHz RF EMF, pulse modulated at 14 Hz or 217 Hz, and a sham control condition. Both active conditions had a peak spatial specific absorption rate of 2 W kg(-1) ... Electroencephalographic power in the spindle frequency range was increased during non-rapid eye movement sleep (2nd episode) following the 14-Hz pulse-modulated condition. A similar but non-significant increase was also observed following the 217-Hz pulse-modulated condition. Importantly, this exposure-induced effect showed considerable individual variability. Regarding cognitive performance, no clear exposure-related effects were seen." Also: "Results also suggest that pulse modulation of the signal is crucial to induce these effects."
- **Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy, Czeisler, PNAS 112:1232, 2015 (abstract and a PNAS commentary).** Reading a light-emitting device before bed, against a printed book: sleep onset delayed by about ten minutes, REM sleep reduced and delayed, melatonin suppressed, the circadian clock shifted later, next-morning alertness lower.
- **Danker-Hopfe, Dorn, Sauter, Schmid, Eggert, Environmental Research 183:109181, 2020, funded by Germany's Federal Office for Radiation Protection (abstract read whole).** "With a double-blind, randomized, sham-controlled cross-over design effects of two RF-EMF exposures (GSM900 and TETRA) on sleep were investigated in samples of 30 elderly healthy male and 30 healthy elderly female volunteers ... a total of nine study nights per participant. Exposure was delivered for 30 min prior to sleep and for the whole night (7.5 h) by a head worn antenna specifically designed for the projects. The peak spatial absorption rate averaged over time in head tissues (psSAR10g) was 6 W/kg for TETRA and 2 W/kg for GSM900 ... exposure to GSM900 and/or TETRA resulted in a significant reduction in arousals, a shorter latency to sleep stage N3, and a shorter self-reported time awake after sleep in both males and females ... Latency to sleep stage R was shorter in females and tended to be longer in males under both exposures ... the number of awakenings was lower only in females and tended to be higher in males under GSM exposure."
- **Sousouri et al., medRxiv, December 2024; NeuroImage 2025 (secondary summaries).** Thirty-four volunteers genotyped for the calcium-channel variant CACNA1C rs7304986; 30 minutes of 700 MHz or 3.6 GHz, the 5G bands, or sham before sleep; 3.6 GHz made sleep spindles faster in the T/C carriers; 700 MHz showed nothing comparable. Small and preliminary; the first study at real 5G frequencies.
- **The awakening literature (a 2024/2025 review of 69 awakening studies, PMC12053782, and older summaries).** Dream recall after waking from REM averages about 82 percent across 35 studies from 1953 to 2000, from non-REM about 43 percent; recall is lower when woken by an alarm than by being called; lower over multi-day studies; sensitive to the sleep environment.
- **Bawin and Adey, PNAS 73:1999, 1976 (abstract).** Weak low-frequency electric fields at 6 and 16 Hz (thresholds about 10 and 56 V/m) changed calcium efflux from chick and cat brain tissue by 12 to 15 percent. Blackman et al., Radiation Research 1982 (abstract): 50 and 147 MHz carriers amplitude-modulated at 16 Hz enhanced the efflux. Replications are mixed; no whole-animal consequence has been shown.
- **National Toxicology Program, TR-595 and TR-596, November 2018 (the NTP's own abstract and level-of-evidence pages).** Male rats exposed whole-body to 900 MHz GSM or CDMA at 1.5, 3 or 6 W/kg, ten minutes on and ten off for just over nine hours a day for two years: "clear evidence of carcinogenic activity," malignant schwannomas of the heart; female rats equivocal; mice little. The lowest level equals today's maximum permitted local exposure from a handset; the signals were 2G and 3G.
- **Karipidis et al., Environment International, September 2024, commissioned by the WHO (secondary summaries).** Sixty-three studies, 1994 to 2022: no increased risk of brain cancer from mobile phone use, including more than ten years of use. Disputed by the advocacy group ICBE-EMF.
- **Ofcom, 2020 to 2021 (the regulator's own pages).** Twenty-two 5G sites in ten UK cities: the highest reading in the 5G band 0.039 percent of the ICNIRP limit; the highest total across all services at a site about 1.5 percent.
- **Pan, Ren, Lindorfer, Wilson, Choffnes, "Panoptispy," Proceedings on Privacy Enhancing Technologies 2018 (summaries and the paper's own front matter).** 17,260 Android apps: none sent audio out and none turned on the microphone without a prompt; several recorded the screen and sent it to analytics firms.
- **Arp, Quiring, Wressnegger, Rieck, "Privacy Threats through Ultrasonic Side Channels on Mobile Devices," IEEE EuroS&P 2017 (summaries).** 234 Android apps carrying the SilverPush kit that keeps the microphone listening for inaudible advertising beacons; no beacons found in TV streams from seven countries; the FTC sent warning letters to twelve developers in March 2016.
- **Tang, LeBel, Jain, Huth, Nature Neuroscience, 1 May 2023 (the NIH summary and the abstract).** Three people, sixteen hours each in an fMRI scanner listening to stories, a decoder trained per person; the output "captures the gist"; "this language decoding requires subject cooperation" and can be consciously resisted.
- **DARPA N3, 2018 on (DARPA's announcements and a Battelle press release).** Nonsurgical read-and-write interfaces as proof of concept; no peer-reviewed phase results public; purpose-built hardware.

## 3. The plain reading

What the record contains, on its face: a government line on putting sound into a head with pulsed radio that runs from 1962 to at least 2000 and is partly classified; a Soviet clinical device that put people to sleep with pulsed radio, light and sound, patented in the United States in 1973; twenty years of microwaves on an embassy whose purpose was never understood and whose one health study found nothing; a 1957 dusting of American cities with a metal compound; an Army study correlating the sky's electromagnetic weather with its viewers' scores; laboratory evidence that pulse-modulated radio at the head shifts sleep spindles and REM timing, and that a night of it at 2 to 6 watts per kilogram makes sleep deeper and less interrupted; two leaks showing what intelligence agencies do to phones: listen, locate, power, hide; a shipped radar in bedrooms that reads breathing; and a patent record, 1976 to 2022, of claims to read brains by radio, none demonstrated.

What the record does not contain, anywhere: a device, document, leak or measurement showing a phone or any radio reading a thought, or a dream being blocked on purpose; or the doses that move sleep in a laboratory being delivered by a phone on a nightstand.

## 4. The working frame, kept apart

Read as if the record hid something, the seams are in `02_THE_SEAMS.md`: the thousand-fold gap between the 1976 American and Soviet exposure standards and who paid for the research; the researchers who lost their contracts; a Phase II that reported feasibility and then went dark; an embassy signal whose purpose was never explained; an Army study that found correlations with the sky and whose results pages this pass did not read; and the twenty-five-year calendar that keeps everything after 2000 shut. ## 5. Where it ends: a measurement

Nothing on this page can settle whether a phone beside a bed changes what a person dreams, because no study has put a phone beside a bed and counted dreams. `03_THE_TEST.md` is a three-arm protocol anyone can run in their own bedroom in three weeks, with the dose arithmetic that places a nightstand on the same scale as the laboratory antennas.
